# Roscoe 68030: replacing the core CPLDs with an FPGA

Working design notes, 21 Nov 2026. Nothing has been built yet, so every pin assignment below can still change.

**Updated 21 Nov 2026 (second session).** The first version was written from the V2 netlist and INTCTRL rev 16, without the CS1–CS3 changes now running on the board (`docs/changes.md`). Four decisions were added after reviewing it against them (section 2, "Added after the CS1–CS3 review"):
- the FPGA owns all device-cycle timing, and IOCTRL becomes a registered decoder (section 10);
- IDE IORDY is kept, on Au pin B29;
- CS1's expansion rules are kept: cards end their cycles with `nEXP_ACK`, and a bus timeout catches empty card space;
- the CS2 fast IDE window is kept.

A second pin pass the same day freed bank-header pins (section 2, "Pin changes"):
- nRMC, HALT, FLASH_WE, nEXP_SEL and the DRAM buffer DIR (nDRAM_DATA_EN) left the FPGA;
- the eight interrupt inputs moved to the Au's LED0–7 pins on the control header;
- BERR moved to U_MEM2, push-pull, so U_OD is gone;
- the debug NMI button was dropped;
- the power controller's request came in on J8-9 (A47), as a ninth interrupt input;
- the SRAM burst signals came in: CBREQ, CBACK and the burst address SRAM_BA2/BA3 (`docs/SRAMBurstProposal.md`).

That leaves 10 of the 102 usable bank-header pins spare (section 6). The sections below are updated to match.

Writing IOCTRL's CUPL the same day changed its plan in four places, all agreed (section 2, "IOCTRL design"; section 10):
- the power-off and software-reset register moved into the FPGA, which sends IOCTRL commands (codes 12–14);
- the CPU's AS comes into IOCTRL, on pin 33, so read selects and strobes end with the bus cycle;
- IOCTRL drives nSYS_RESET high for two clocks as it releases it;
- nCPU_RD is held high until the FPGA's DONE.

On 22 Nov 2026 the interrupt levels and vectors were agreed, and the interrupt controller was written in Verilog with a self-checking testbench (section 11, "HDL"). The same day the FPGA's clocking was decided (CPU_CLK, both edges) and the bus controller and DRAM controller were written, with CBR refresh every 15.6 µs instead of rev 31's 8 µs (section 16). Only the SRAM's synchronous cycles and bursts, and the redesigned PTC, are still missing.

## 1. Summary

The plan replaces four of Roscoe's programmable parts with one FPGA board:

| Today | Part | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| BUSCTRL (U58) | ATF1508 | FPGA |
| DEVMAP / DEVDCD (U59) | ATF1508 | FPGA, except the slow-device strobes |
| INTCTRL (U60) | ATF1508 | FPGA |
| PTC (U11) + its oscillator (U12) | ATF1504 | FPGA (8-bit PTC, timers count from CPU_CLK) |

- **FPGA board:** Alchitry Au V2 (Xilinx Artix-7 XC7A35T), mounted on an Alchitry Br Wide V2 breakout. The breakout plugs into the mainboard through 0.1" headers.
- **One small CPLD stays:** an ATF1504 (TQFP-44), called **IOCTRL**. It turns the FPGA's 4-bit device code and enable into the slow 5V device selects and strobes, registered on CPU_CLK, while the FPGA decides every edge. It also holds reset until the FPGA is configured, and keeps the power-off request bit. The source is `fpga/ioctrl/IOCTRL_REV1.PLD` (section 10).
- **Memory stays at 5V:** DRAM SIMMs and the SRAM board are unchanged. The FPGA reaches them, and the rest of the 5V board, through buffers; it never touches 5V directly.
- **Design clock:** 16 MHz CPU_CLK, as the board runs today. The timings in sections 10 and 12 are worked for it; faster clocks are a later option.

## 2. Decision log

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| Keep DRAM and SRAM at 5V | The SIMMs and the SRAM board exist and work. The FPGA only needs address and control, plus 8 data lines. |
| Translate every FPGA pin | None of the candidate FPGAs is 5V tolerant (abs max input: Gowin GW2A 3.75V; Efinix Trion 4.6V; Artix-7 HR banks VCCO + 0.55V). |
| Alchitry Au V2 + Br Wide | Most 3.3V I/O of the boards looked at (102 usable), mature tools (Vivado), USB JTAG + UART built in, and easy 0.1" connection to the mainboard. Tang Primer 20K and a home-made Trion T20 card were evaluated and dropped (section 14). |
| FPGA takes BUSCTRL, DEVMAP, INTCTRL and PTC | Interrupt controller judged more important than the PTC. Once the FPGA has D24–D31 for interrupt vectors, the PTC costs no extra pins. |
| Keep one CPLD (ATF1504) | Driving the 16 slow-device strobes directly would take 11 more FPGA pins than the 5-pin device code, and only 3 are spare. Most of those strobes land on AHC/HC inputs (74AHC244 IDE buffers, 74HC273 latches) with CMOS thresholds (VIH 3.5V at 5V), which 3.3V can't drive. Reset must also work while the FPGA is still loading. The 1504 is easier to solder than the 1508, and its TQFP-44 footprint is already on the board (U11). |
| FPGA gets 8 data lines (D24–D31) | Needed for interrupt vectors, mask registers and the PTC. It also opens up FPGA registers in general, for example software-set expansion window widths. |
| PTC becomes an 8-bit device | Shares D24–D31 with the interrupt controller. |
| No expansion select | The five old bussed selects (8/16/32-bit device, VIO, VMEM) go, and so does the single nEXP_SEL first planned in their place ("Pin changes" below). Cards decode their own window from EXP_A0–A31, and end their cycles with `nEXP_ACK` (A26), as CS1 defined (see below). |
| Port width from the memory map | The FPGA sizes DSACK per expansion window. It can now be a register, since the FPGA sees D24–D31. |
| 8 interrupt inputs | IDE ×2, UART ×2, RTC, expansion ×3, on the Au's LED0–7 pins (section 11). The NIC interrupt is dropped, video uses a slot interrupt, and the PTC interrupts are internal. The debug button (NMI) is dropped. The power controller's request is a ninth input, on J8-9 ("Pin changes" below). |
| Drop nEXP_CACHE_CTRL | Not needed. |
| nCPU_RD = NOT CPU_RW, made in IOCTRL | Frees an FPGA pin. Loads are SRAM/flash /OE and the UART /IOR. The IDE gets its own DIOR (section 10). BUSCTRL rev 29 had delayed nCPU_RD to S3 only because the IDE shared it: /IOR ran ahead of /CS, which breaks ATA t1 (TheoryOfOperations 12.5). With the IDE on its own DIOR that reason is gone (open item 6, closed). |
| nFPU_CS stays in the FPGA (**reversed** 22 Nov 2026: see "FPGA HDL" below) | A direct decode in the CPLD was considered (faster, frees a pin) but rejected. The FPGA decodes FPU space internally anyway. The timing pass then showed the path through the FPGA was too slow. |
| New nDRAM_BUF_OE | The DRAM data buffers (74HCT245 U29–U32) have /OE tied to GND today. Giving the FPGA control of /OE removes the bus-contention hazard, and lets DIR simply follow CPU R/W ("Pin changes" below). |
| DEV_WAIT pin removed | The old BUSCTRL↔DEVMAP device handshake (nDEV_WAIT, nDEV_ACK since CS1) is internal now. The FPGA times every device cycle itself and stretches IDE base-window cycles while IORDY is low (see below). |
| Expansion spare lines dropped | EXP_DM_CPLD1/2 and EXP_INT_CPLD1/2 have no defined use. EXP_BC_CPLD1/2 (B26/B30) are CS1's VMEM busy/grant pair (`changes.md` 1.4). They were to stay reserved on the connector, with B26's 4.7K pull-up; since 22 Nov 2026 they are unused, as no card uses them (below). Besides the slots, these lines feed the CLK1/CLK2 pins of the logic-analyzer headers U69, U23 and U24, which lose those clocks. |
| CLK_DIV16 internal | It only clocked the PTC. |

**Added after the CS1–CS3 review (agreed 21 Nov 2026)**

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| The FPGA owns all device-cycle timing; IOCTRL is a registered decoder | The first plan counted each device's timing twice: wait states in the FPGA (for DSACK) and strobe edges in IOCTRL (from CPU_CLK). The two drift apart by a clock whenever nDEV_EN reaches IOCTRL close to a falling edge, which is only about 5 ns away at 20 MHz. Now IOCTRL registers DEV_CODE, nDEV_EN and R/W on every falling CPU_CLK edge, and has no counters. DEV_CODE alone sets each select window, and nDEV_EN sets the strobe or phase window inside it. Every edge is the FPGA's decision, so a late AS costs at most a clock and the two parts can never disagree. Each IOCTRL output is a flop, so a code changing several bits between edges cannot glitch a latch or a chip select. Details and timings: section 10. |
| Keep IDE IORDY | CS3 has run IORDY since late 2026. J4/J6 pin 27 are joined, with a 1K pull-up to +5V, and the line reaches the FPGA through U_IN5's spare input on Au pin B29 (then the last free pin). While IORDY is low the FPGA freezes the base-window schedule. The fast window can't honour it, as in CS3. |
| Keep CS1's expansion rules | A26 is `nEXP_ACK`: a card pulls it low when its cycle may end. With no acknowledge, the cycle runs into the bus timeout and ends in BERR, so empty card space is caught. The card rules are in `changes.md` 1.6. R10 becomes 1K. The expansion interrupts stay; since 22 Nov 2026 they are three lines bussed to every slot. (The single `nEXP_SEL` was dropped later, in "Pin changes".) B26/B30 were reserved for a later VMEM hold-off; since 22 Nov 2026 they are unused. Without them, the VMEM window is ordinary card space ended by `nEXP_ACK`, at one wait state or more at 16 MHz. |
| Keep the CS2 fast IDE window | The registers also appear at base + 0x800 (A11), and the Data register there runs at PIO mode 2. Since the FPGA times every edge, this costs no device codes: the FPGA decodes A11 and picks the faster schedule (section 10). Codes 13–15 stay spare. |

**Pin changes (agreed 21 Nov 2026)**

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| Drop nRMC | None of the old CPLDs used it (the net only reaches BUSCTRL pin 7), and nothing else ever masters the bus. |
| Drop the HALT output | BUSCTRL never drove HALT low. R2 keeps the line high, and U1 still drives the HALT LED from it, so a double bus fault still shows. |
| BERR push-pull through U_MEM2 | With HALT gone, U_OD (74LVC2G07) would have carried BERR alone. BUSCTRL drives BERR push-pull today, and only the CPU and the logic-analyzer header U4 share the net, so a spare U_MEM2 channel does it: no U_OD, no new pull-up, and a fast negation edge. BERR's FPGA pin moved to J14-4, next to U_MEM2. |
| Flash /WE = CPU R/W | Flash writes become CE#-controlled, as the SRAM board's already are (its nWR is CPU R/W). The SST39SF040 allows this (data sheet table 12: TCS = TCH = 0). The FPGA's timing rules are in section 12. Today BUSCTRL makes /WE from the flash decode and R/W, so it falls and rises with /CE anyway. |
| Drop nEXP_SEL | Each card decodes its own window from EXP_A0–A31 and AS. The slots have no FC lines, but no CPU-space cycle reaches 0x3–0x7: interrupt acknowledge puts all ones on the address bus, and breakpoint and coprocessor cycles use 0x0000_xxxx–0x0002_xxxx (BUSCTRL rev 31's decode comments). The five old select pins and A28 become free on the connector (section 15). |
| DRAM buffer DIR = CPU R/W | U29–U32 are 74HCT245s with the CPU on their B side. BUSCTRL drives DIR high only for DRAM reads. Once nDRAM_BUF_OE gates /OE, DIR = R/W gives the same direction whenever the buffers are on. |
| Interrupts on the Au's LED0–7 pins | The control header brings out eight bank-14 pins that also drive the Au's LEDs (330Ω to GND). Driven by U_IN6 through 100Ω, they take the eight interrupt inputs and free eight bank-header pins, and the LEDs show the raw interrupt lines. The control headers have no ground, which is fine for slow lines. |
| No debug NMI button | Not needed. The Au's RESET pin (P6, Br J17-4) stays unconnected. |
| Power-controller request into the FPGA | The AVR (U65) asks for a coordinated shutdown with a 50 ms high pulse on PWR_REQGR, the POWER_CONTROL_OUT net. U_IN5's spare ch8 brings it to J8-9 (A47). The line changes only a few times per power cycle, so it suits the hole below the clock, and a 1K series resistor softens those edges. IOCTRL keeps only the power-off bit, so its CUPL needs no data-bus output. |
| SRAM burst signals | For 2-1-1-1 cache-line fills from the SRAM, as `docs/SRAMBurstProposal.md` worked out for the CPLD board, and which it expected the next board to wire up. The 68030 holds the address constant through a burst, so SRAM_BA2/BA3 replace CPU A2/A3 on riser pins A18/B18 and count modulo 4. CBACK goes through U_CPU, timed like STERM. With CBACK on a pin, the FPGA can hold bursts off until they are proven, and refuse them from any STERM device that can't burst. CBREQ, on U_IN5's last input, is optional: a burst simply ends when AS negates. It does tell the FPGA in S1 that a fill will be a burst. U_CPU becomes a 74LVC244A to give CBACK a channel. |

**IOCTRL design (agreed 21 Nov 2026)**

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| The FPGA keeps the power-off/software-reset register; IOCTRL takes commands (codes 12–14) | IOCTRL needs no data lines, so D24/D25 leave it and two pins are freed. Software sees the same bits. The FPGA can make the register readable, and knows when the CPU itself drives POWER_CONTROL_OUT high. |
| nCPU_AS into IOCTRL (pin 33) | In the first plan a UART read select ran into the next cycle's S0. If that cycle is a write, the UART sees /CS with /IOW (CPU R/W) low and takes a spurious write, and its address changes under an open read. The UART has no data hold (tDD min 0), so ending on the latch edge isn't safe either. With AS, the select and every other read strobe or latch strobe ends when the cycle does, as nCPU_RD's AS preset does on the board today. |
| IOCTRL drives nSYS_RESET high as it releases it | The 68030's RESET input must change within 1.5 clocks (#32). With only R47 (10K), an open-drain release takes microseconds. IOCTRL drives the line low in reset, high for two clocks at release, then leaves it Hi-Z so the RESET instruction still works. |
| nCPU_RD held high while DONE is low | While 3V3_FPGA is down, the AHCT244s assert every SRAM and flash select. With /OE held off, none of them can drive the data bus. This closes the power-up window (section 12) without the spare pin. |
| Pull-ups on AS and R/W, pull-down on DONE | The board's part in the AS and DONE rows above. The 68030 three-states AS and R/W during reset, and the AS-tied outputs rely on AS being high then. A 100K pull-down makes a missing Au read as unconfigured. |

**FPGA HDL (agreed 22 Nov 2026)**

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| Clocking: CPU_CLK straight in, both edges | It's how the CPLDs work, section 10's schedule is written in those edges, and no MMCM is needed now. A 4× MMCM clock wouldn't save a wait state at 16 MHz: AS-to-DSACK is the same path, and DRAM is held at 1 wait state by the RAS access time through the HCT245s. The SRAM burst counter will add an MMCM output for itself (section 12). |
| CBR refresh every 15.6 µs | Jeff's intent. Rev 31 actually refreshes every 128 clocks (8 µs at 16 MHz), because its timer counts CPU_CLK/8, not the CPU_CLK/16 its comments describe (`docs/DRAM.md` 4.1). 15.6 µs suits 2K/32 ms and 4K/64 ms parts. 8K/64 ms parts, which some 64 Mbit chips on 128 MB SIMMs are, need 7.8 µs: it's the `REFRESH_NS` parameter (section 16). |
| The PTC is redesigned, not ported | Jeff's plan. The bus controller came first. |
| nFPU_CS from a 74AHCT138 on the 5V side (U_FPUCS) | Through U_IN, the FPGA and U_MEM2, the select could stay asserted up to about 20 ns into the next cycle's AS, worst case. The 68882 manual calls that incorrect. Qualifying CS with AS, or registering it, breaks the 68882's CS-to-DS setup on writes (#8B). One '138 on the CPU's own lines gives Y7 = nFPU_CS at about rev 31's speed: FC0–2 on the selects, A17 on G1, A19 and A16 on /G2A and /G2B. It frees J8-12 and U_MEM2 ch3 (sections 9 and 16). An ATF22V10C-10 would also meet the 68882's CS hold on paper, but it draws 85–130 mA, so Jeff kept the '138. |

**Expansion connector and board (agreed 22 Nov 2026)**

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| Three expansion interrupt lines, each bussed to every slot | A card that does two things can use two interrupts (Jeff). They are today's B21, A23 and A24 (nEXP_INT3A, 3B, 2A), already bussed and pulled up, and each keeps its old mask bit (section 11). This replaces the earlier plan of one pin routed separately to each slot. |
| The five old select pins reserved; the other old control lines unused | Jeff: keep A20, A21, A30, B19 and B20 free in case cards need them. The four other interrupt pins, A28 and the old CPLD lines (A31, B26, B29, B30, B31) are unused, and lines for needs not yet known can be added later (section 15, item 13). A21 and A30 then came back as selects (next table). |
| Female sockets on Roscoe, pins on the Br | Jeff's choice. The stack-up in the Br Wide footprint page assumes it. |
| RAS wiring as today | `ras_n[0..7]` are SIMM 1–4 rank A, then SIMM 1–4 rank B, the address order of BUSCTRL rev 31's nDRAM_S1A … S4B (pins 19–25, 27). Rank A is a SIMM's RAS0 and RAS2 (pins 44, 34), rank B its RAS1 and RAS3 (pins 45, 33). So nRAS0–7 wire up as today. |
| Boot-flash reads at 1 wait state | Agreed (rev 31 used 0). The FPGA's registered CE comes about 10 ns later than BUSCTRL's (section 16). |

**Register map and card windows (agreed 22 Nov 2026)**

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| FPGA registers at 0x3000_0200–020F | Masks (0x200/0x201), power and reset (0x202) and the interrupt requests (0x204/0x205) stay or go where proposed. New: the design version at 0x203 (read only), a control register at 0x206 (SRAM synchronous cycles and bursts, both off at reset, for bring-up) and a scratch register at 0x207 for bus tests. 0x208–0x20F stay free (section 16). |
| The new PTC at 0x3000_0400 | It is an 8-bit device now, so it moves into the 8-bit region. Its layout is Jeff's redesign; the window acknowledges at once until then. |
| 0x3: on-board devices at the start, the rest 8-bit card space | UARTs, FPGA registers, RTC, PTC and the latches stay in 0x3000_0000–0x3000_08FF. Everything else in 0x3, the VIO window 0x3000_05xx included, is 8-bit card space ended by nEXP_ACK. Before, the empty on-board windows were acknowledged at once. |
| 0x4: unchanged | IDE in 0x4000_0000–0x40FF_FFFF, 16-bit card space at 0x4100_0000–0x4FFF_FFFF. |
| 0x5: all 32-bit card space, its first 16 MB the VMEM window | Jeff widened VMEM from 2 MB to 16 MB (0x5000_0000–0x50FF_FFFF). |
| 0x6–0x7 stay a bus error | For now. |
| The FPGA drives nEXP_VMEM_CS (A21) and nEXP_VIO_CS (A30) again | The VGA card as drawn can't decode its own windows: A20–A31 don't connect on it, and A2–A19 only reach its video-memory buffers. Both selects come on at R1 and go off as AS negates, through U_MEM2's last two channels (ch3, ch8) from J8-12 and J8-14. A20, B19 and B20 stay reserved. |
| Port widths fixed by region | A card picks the region that matches its bus width, and nothing needs setting up at boot. A width register could go in 0x208+ if a card ever needs one. |

## 3. What lives where

| Function | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Address decode, memory map, DSACK/STERM/BERR/CIIN | FPGA | DSACK three-stated with active negation (shared with the 68882). HALT is left to the CPU and R2. |
| DRAM controller (RAS0–7, CAS0–3, WE, mux, data-buffer /OE) | FPGA | Refresh too. The buffer DIR is CPU R/W. |
| SRAM and flash selects, byte enables | FPGA | Writes to both are CE#-controlled: each takes /WE straight from CPU R/W. |
| SRAM synchronous cycles and burst fills | FPGA | STERM reads in 2 clocks, writes in 3, cache-line fills 2-1-1-1 (`docs/SRAMBurstProposal.md`). The FPGA's BA3:BA2 counter replaces CPU A3:A2 at the SRAM. |
| Card selects nEXP_VMEM_CS, nEXP_VIO_CS | FPGA | For cards that don't decode the address themselves (the VGA card): 0x5000_0000–0x50FF_FFFF and 0x3000_05xx, on connector A21 and A30. |
| 68882 chip select | U_FPUCS (74AHCT138, 5V side) | Decoded from the CPU's own FC0–2, A17, A19 and A16, as fast as rev 31's CPLD (section 9). The FPGA still never drives DSACK in FPU cycles, and must BERR an unanswered one so the 68030 takes the F-line exception. |
| Interrupt controller (9 inputs, IPL0–2, vectors) | FPGA | All interrupts vectored, as today. |
| PTC (timers) | FPGA | 8-bit registers on D24–D31. |
| Expansion width and acknowledge | FPGA | No select line: cards decode EXP_A0–A31. The card ends the cycle with nEXP_ACK (A26); the bus timeout catches empty card space. |
| Device-cycle timing (wait states, IDE base and fast windows, IORDY, RTC phases) | FPGA | One sequencer drives DEV_CODE, nDEV_EN and DSACK together (section 10). |
| Slow-device selects and strobes (IDE, RTC, UART, 7-seg, status LEDs) | IOCTRL (ATF1504) | Registered decode of DEV_CODE[3:0] + nDEV_EN on falling CPU_CLK edges. No timing of its own. |
| Reset hold, AVR reset merge, software reset | IOCTRL | Holds nSYS_RESET while the FPGA's DONE is low, then for 1024 clocks. The software reset is code 14. |
| Power-off request bit (to the AVR) | IOCTRL | Set and cleared by FPGA commands (codes 12/13). The register software sees is in the FPGA. |
| nCPU_RD, UART reset | IOCTRL | Simple 5V logic. |

## 4. Alchitry Au V2: what the datasheets and schematic say

**FPGA and tools**
- XC7A35T-2FTG256 (an XC7A50T version is also listed). Vivado runs on a free license.
- 104 I/O on two 80-pin bank headers (Bank A, Bank B), plus a 50-pin control header. The connectors are Hirose DF40 at 0.4 mm pitch, with a 4 mm stack height.
- On the bank headers, every third row is a ground pair (pins 1/2, 7/8, 13/14, …).

**Bank voltages** (from the Au V2 schematic and pinout sheet)

| Au pins | FPGA bank | Voltage |
|---|---|---|
| Bank A, all 52 I/O | 35, plus 4 pins of bank 14 (A3–A6) | 3.3V fixed |
| Bank B, 3–30, 34/36, 39–42, 45/47 | 14 | 3.3V fixed |
| Bank B 33/35 | 35 | 3.3V, shared with the on-board Qwiic connector (no pull-ups on the Au) |
| Bank B 46/48 | 15 | **1.35V** (DDR3L bank); unusable here |
| Bank B 51–78 (20 pins) | 34 | Selectable 3.3/2.5/1.8V; 3.3V when VBSEL_A and VBSEL_B float |

**Clock-capable pins**
- MRCC: A39/A41 (35_L12), A45/A47 (35_L13), B45/B47 (14_L13).
- SRCC: A40/A42 (35_L11), A46/A48 (35_L14), B39/B41 (14_L14), B40/B42 (14_L11).
- A single-ended clock goes on the P pin. **CPU_CLK is on A41 (35_L12_MRCC_P, ball D4).**

**Power**
- The board runs from 5–12V on VCC/RAW. Roscoe's +5V can feed it through the control header.
- USB VBUS goes through an LM73100 ideal-diode switch, so USB can stay plugged in while Roscoe powers the board.
- An ADP5052 PMIC makes the rails. The 3.3V rail is rated 4A and is brought out on the control header (+3V3), so it can power the 74LVC translators and pull-ups. Powered from it, the translators are off whenever the FPGA is, so they can't back-drive it.
- On the Au's control header, +3V3 is on the odd pins 1–15 and RAW on the even pins 2–16. The Br brings one of each out to a hole (J17-1, J18-1).

**Configuration and debug**
- Master SPI boot from on-board QSPI flash. CFGBVS is tied to VCCO (set `CFGBVS VCCO`, `CONFIG_VOLTAGE 3.3` in the XDC).
- **PUDC_B (L15) is tied to 3.3V**, which turns off the internal pull-ups during configuration. Every user pin floats while the FPGA loads, so the external pull-ups on active-low outputs are required.
- DONE and PROGRAM_B each have a 4.7K pull-up on the Au, and both reach the control header. DONE also drives the DONE LED, through a 2N7002 (Q2B) whose gate has another 10K pull-up (R53). So DONE sees about 3.2K to 3.3V and no DC load (Au schematic sheet 6).
- An FT2232 on USB gives JTAG and a UART on dedicated FPGA pins: USB_RX = P15 (FPGA input), USB_TX = P16 (FPGA output). No header pins are needed for debug.

**I/O standard**
- LVCMOS33 with TTL-level inputs. Not 5V tolerant.

- The Br Wide V2 was $14.99. Its header kit (four 2×14 and two 2×6 headers) was $9.99.

## 5. Alchitry Br Wide V2 breakout

Sources: `BrWideSchematic.pdf` (Br Wide v4) for the wiring, and Alchitry's 3D model `BrWide.step` for the mechanics.

- **Mechanics:** 70 × 55 mm, with 1.5 mm corner chamfers and a notch 32 mm wide and 5.5 mm deep in one long edge.
  - The DF40 plugs are on the underside and the receptacles on top. So the Au plugs in from below and hangs 4.0 mm under the Br.
  - The hole fields sit outside the Au's 55 × 45 mm outline, which is what the Wide version is for.
  - On Roscoe the Au therefore sits between the Br and the main board. Keep the parts under it low, and leave room for a cable at its USB-C. The Au's four Ø2.2 mounting holes pass through the Br too.
  - The notch sits over the Au's LEDs (now the interrupt lines) and its reset button.
- **Hole positions:** given in the pattern frame: looking down with the silkscreen side up, the logo edge at the top, the origin at the outline box's lower-left corner, in mm.
  - Bank B's four columns sit at X 2.06–9.68 on the left. Bank A's and Control's sit at X 60.26–67.94 on the right.
  - Bank hole 1 is at Y 35.26 and control hole 1 at Y 51.77, with holes stepping down 2.54.
  - Columns are 2.54 apart in Bank B and 2.56 apart in Bank A and Control, per the model.
  - The mounting holes are at (15.00, 2.50), (15.00, 52.50), (55.00, 2.50) and (55.00, 52.50).
  - Every hole is in `BrWide_pinlist.csv`, checked against all 136 holes in the model.
- **Roscoe pin numbers:** one number per hole, 1–136, used for both the schematic symbol and the PCB pattern.
  - The rule: pin = 14 × (J − 7) + hole for J7–J14, and 112 + 6 × (J − 17) + hole for J17–J20.
  - Bank A is pins 1–56, Bank B 57–112, Control 113–136. In each field, columns run left to right and holes top to bottom.
  - Pin 1 is J7-1, a ground. CPU_CLK (J8-8) is pin 22.
- **Schematic symbol** (`Roscoe_BrWide.elixml`): one part per field.
  - A field's first two headers run down the left side and its last two down the right: J7/J8 and J9/J10, J11/J12 and J13/J14, J17/J18 and J19/J20.
  - Holes run top to bottom, under a label row that names the header. Every pin end is on the 0.1" grid.
  - Pin names are the nets, with these exceptions: spare holes are SPARE_<Au pin>, except J7-8, which is NC_A39. B46/B48 are NC_1V35. The control header's VCC, VBSEL and "+2.5V" holes are VCC_RAW, VBSEL_A and VBSEL_B.
  - Pin types: FPGA inputs, outputs and I/O get those ERC types, and the supply pins get Power.
- **Silkscreen:** each bank hole is labelled with its Au pin number (G for ground). The control holes carry short names: V+, R, DN, AV, L0–L7, RS, PR, VS, 2.5, the JTAG pin numbers, and AP, AN, AR, AG.

- **Pass-through:** the Br carries both DF40 genders wired in parallel: receptacles J2 (50-pin), J3 and J4 (80-pin), all 4.0 mm stack-height parts, and plugs J1, J5 and J6. So other Alchitry boards can still stack with it, and every signal also reaches its 0.1" holes.
- **Bank headers:** eight 14-hole columns, J7–J14, filled by the kit's four 2×14 headers.
  - **Hole 1 of every column is GND.**
  - Holes 2–14 carry Au pins in steps of 6:
    - J7: A3, A9 … A75. J8: A5 … A77. J9: A4 … A76. J10: A6 … A78.
    - J11–J14 are the same pattern for Bank B.
- **Control headers:** four 6-hole columns, J17–J20, filled by the kit's two 2×6 headers. They carry one +3V3 pin, one VCC pin, DONE, PROGRAM_B, JTAG, the LED-shared pins, RESET, the VBSEL pins and the analog pins. **Apart from AGND (the analog ground), they have no ground.**
  - The eight LED-shared pins are ordinary bank-14 FPGA pins (K13, K12, L14, L13, M15, M14, M12, P14 for LED0–7). On the Au each one also drives an LED through 330Ω to GND; the header connects on the ball side of the resistor. Roscoe uses them for the interrupt inputs.
- **Grounds:** only 8 ground holes for 104 signals. That's about 12 of our signals per ground, against a ground pair every third row on the Au's own connectors. This is the weak spot of the 0.1" interface (section 12).
- **Label quirk:** the Br labels J20-4 "+2.5V", but it's the Au V2's control-header pin 40, VBSEL_B. Leave it, and J19-4 (VBSEL_A, Au pin 38), unconnected.

These were freed on 21 Nov 2026 by dropping nRMC, HALT, FLASH_WE, nEXP_SEL and nDRAM_DATA_EN, moving BERR to J14-4, and moving the interrupt inputs to the control header (section 2, "Pin changes"). J8-9 (A47), directly below the clock, then took the power request: a static line, through 1K (section 9). J8-6, J10-3, J12-4 and J14-6 then took the SRAM burst signals. On 22 Nov 2026 the timing pass moved nDSACK0, nCIIN, DEV_CODE0–3 and nDEV_EN into bank 35 next to AS, and A8–A15 and A24 out of their way (section 16). That used J10-4, J12-7, J12-13 and J12-14, and freed J9-2 and J8-11..13. Later that day nFPU_CS and FC1, then SRAM_BA2/3, moved into bank 35 beside their inputs. That used J8-11..13 and J10-14 and freed J8-2, J12-4, J14-5 and J14-6, all in bank 14. Then nFPU_CS left the FPGA for U_FPUCS, a 74AHCT138 (section 9), and J8-12 was free again. The card selects nEXP_VMEM_CS and nEXP_VIO_CS then took J8-12 and J8-14 (section 16).

Spare translator channels: U_CPU ch8 (always driven; it can buffer a 5V input too) and U_MEM2 ch8 (a 5V output). U_IN5 and U_DRAM2 are full. Candidate uses are in section 15.

**Layout logic**
- **Where the FPGA's banks are:** Bank A (J7–J10) is FPGA bank 35, in the die's right-hand I/O column (rows 50–99), with AS at row 70. The exception is each header's hole 2, which is in bank 14, in the left-hand column on the far side of the die. Bank B (J11–J14) is bank 14 and bank 34 (the right-hand column, rows 30–49), plus the two Qwiic pins in bank 35.
- **CPU side on Bank A:**
  - **What it carries:** the clock, the CPU bus inputs (CBREQ among them), A16–A31, the U_CPU outputs (DSACK, STERM, CIIN, CBACK), the device-code bus, SRAM_BA2/3, and the card selects nEXP_VMEM_CS and nEXP_VIO_CS, which AS ends directly.
  - **Why (22 Nov 2026):** every output the FPGA must assert half a clock after seeing AS sits in bank 35 next to AS. So does every output decoded straight from the address: SRAM_BA2/3 beside A2/A3, and nCIIN beside A28–A31 (section 16). FC0–2 and A16–A19 are there too, for the decode at R1.
  - **U_CPU's holes:** J9/J10 holes 3–5.
  - **Around the clock:** J7-7, J7-8 and J7-9 stay empty, and J8-9 carries only the static power request.
- **Memory side on Bank B:** DRAM control, A8–A15 (U_IN1, J12 holes 7–14), the SRAM and flash selects, BERR and IPL (U_MEM2's other channels, on J14), the data bus, and IORDY.
  - Four translator channels on this side take their signals from Bank A: U_MEM2 ch3 (nEXP_VMEM_CS, J8-12), ch4 (SRAM_BA2, J10-14) and ch8 (nEXP_VIO_CS, J8-14), and U_DRAM2 ch7 (SRAM_BA3, J8-11). Their traces cross the carrier, about 55 mm, which costs well under a nanosecond.
- **Interrupts:** on the control headers, J17–J20 holes 2–3 (below).
- **Swapping:** order inside a group is arbitrary for most signals. But the half-clock outputs (nDSACK0/1, nDSACK_OE, DEV_CODE0–3, nDEV_EN, nCIIN, nSTERM, nCBACK) and AS must stay together in bank 35, and SRAM_BA2/3 with their inputs, and the card selects, with them. After any swap, update the workbook, regenerate the XDC and rerun `hdl/check-hdl.ps1` for timing.

**Rules**
- **3V3_FPGA rail:** the Au's +3V3 (Br J17-1).
- **Why LVC on inputs:** LVC inputs take 5V and have power-off protection (Ioff).
- **Why AHCT on outputs:** AHCT outputs give a full 5V swing. That keeps the existing AHC157 DRAM address muxes (CMOS thresholds) and suits the SIMMs (VIH 2.4V).
- **Driving 5V parts from 3.3V:** 3.3V outputs drive TTL-threshold inputs directly. That covers the 68030 and 68882 inputs (VIH 2.0V), the ATF1504 inputs, and HCT/AHCT/ACT parts. That's why the DEV_CODE bus and DONE go to IOCTRL without a translator.
- **Series resistors:** put 33Ω on every translator output that drives the FPGA. It slows the edges through the 0.1" headers. The eight interrupt lines get 100Ω instead (next rule).
- **Interrupt inputs on the LED pins:** U_IN6 drives the Au's LED0–7 pins through 100Ω each. Each pin also carries an Au LED with 330Ω to GND, about 3 mA when high, so the pin still sits near 3.0V (VIH is 2.0V). The 100Ω also limits the current to about 25 mA should a bitstream drive those pins, as a demo design that blinks the LEDs would. Load the Roscoe bitstream into the Au's flash before fitting it anyway: any bitstream that drives Roscoe's input pins fights the translators.
- **STERM, CIIN and CBACK:** none has a pull-up on the board (CBACK isn't even connected on V2), and BUSCTRL drove STERM and CIIN push-pull. U_CPU's group 2 (/2OE on GND) drives all three, so none is needed.
- **BERR:** driven push-pull by U_MEM2, as BUSCTRL drives it today. Only the CPU and the logic-analyzer header U4 share nCPU_BERR, so it needs no pull-up.
- **68882 select (U_FPUCS, 22 Nov 2026):** a '138 on the CPU's own lines, next to the 68882, replaces the FPGA's nFPU_CS (section 16, "The 68882's select").
  - **Decode:** Y7 is low only when FC = 7 (CPU space), A17 = 1 and A19 = A16 = 0. That's coprocessor cycles (type 2), and type 6, which is reserved and never generated. A18 and the coprocessor ID (A15–A13) aren't decoded, as in rev 31. Leave Y0–Y6 open.
  - **Part:** it needs TTL-level inputs at 5V, so use an AHCT, ACT or F '138, roughly 8–12 ns worst case. Not HCT (about 40 ns) or LS.
    - Prefer one whose sheet guarantees a minimum delay of 2 ns or more. That covers the 68882's 10 ns CS hold on paper (section 16). AHCT's 1 ns minimum is about 1 ns short in the extreme corner.
  - **Not a 22V10:** an ATF22V10C-10 (`datasheets/22V10.pdf`, tPD 3.0–10.0 ns) was considered on 22 Nov 2026. It would decode type 2 exactly and guarantee the CS hold, but it draws 85–130 mA against microamps for the '138, so Jeff kept the '138.
  - **The FPGA's part:** none for the select. It still never drives DSACK in CPU space other than interrupt acknowledge, and the bus timeout still ends an FPU cycle nobody answers.
- **HALT:** no FPGA connection. R2 keeps it high, and U1 still drives the HALT LED from it.
- **DRAM data buffers:** lift U29–U32 /OE (pin 19) off GND and drive it from nDRAM_BUF_OE, and wire DIR (pin 1) to CPU R/W. The CPU is on the B side, so R/W high (a read) drives DRAM data onto the CPU bus, as nDRAM_DATA_EN did.
- **Flash /WE:** wire U3 pin 31 to CPU R/W. Writes become CE#-controlled (section 12).
- **SRAM burst address:** riser pins A18/B18 (the SRAMs' A0/A1) take SRAM_BA2/BA3 instead of CPU A2/A3, so the riser itself doesn't change. Every other A2/A3 load (flash, FPU, UART, the RTC and expansion buffers, U_IN5) stays on the CPU's own lines.
  - Optionally feed the DRAM column mux (U26 1B/2B, column bits 0–1) from BA2/BA3 as well. Outside a burst they equal A3:A2, and the column phase comes long after they settle. A later DRAM page-mode burst needs exactly this (`docs/DRAM.md` section 6), so it would cost no more pins.
- **IORDY:** join J4 pin 27 and J6 pin 27, pull them up to +5V with 1K (the ATA host value, as CS3's P8), and feed U_IN5. Only the selected drive ever drives IORDY, so both ports can share it.
- **Power request:** U_IN5 ch8 takes the POWER_CONTROL_OUT net (the AVR's PWR_REQGR) and drives J8-9 through 1K instead of 33Ω, to soften its few edges next to the clock.
  - Keep R9/R46, the two 10K pull-downs. They hold the line low so that nothing drives the unpowered CPLD (INTCTRL today, IOCTRL next) while the main power is off and the AVR runs on standby.
  - U_IN5 is safe in that state too: LVC inputs have power-off protection (Ioff), so with 3V3_FPGA dead it neither loads the line nor passes anything to the FPGA.
- **nEXP_ACK:** R10 becomes 1K (CS1's P2), so an open-drain card acknowledge releases before the next cycle.
- **DEV_CODE pull-downs:** put 10K pull-downs on DEV_CODE[3:0], so IOCTRL's inputs (pin-keepers off) sit at code 0 instead of floating while the FPGA configures. IOCTRL ignores them anyway until DONE is high; this only keeps the inputs at defined levels.
- **IOCTRL's other pull-ups and pull-downs** (section 10, board requirements): 10K pull-ups to +5V on nCPU_AS and CPU_RW, which the 68030 floats during reset; a 100K pull-down on DONE at IOCTRL; and, optionally, 10K pull-ups on IOCTRL's 16 active-low outputs.

## 10. IOCTRL (ATF1504)

**Status (21 Nov 2026):** written as `fpga/ioctrl/IOCTRL_REV1.PLD`, and its design agreed. It compiles, fits the TQFP-44 with JTAG on and every pin locked, and passes a 3,221-vector simulation (`fpga/ioctrl/check-ioctrl.ps1`). It has not been built or programmed. Writing it changed the plan in four places, marked **(new)** below and listed in section 2, "IOCTRL design".

**How it works.** IOCTRL is a registered decoder with no counters. On every falling CPU_CLK edge it samples DEV_CODE[3:0], nDEV_EN and CPU R/W, and every select and strobe output is a flip-flop clocked on that edge. The FPGA decides on which edge each output changes.
- **DEV_CODE alone sets the select window:** chip select, IDE buffer enable, the RTC's address strobe, latch strobes.
- **nDEV_EN sets the strobe or phase window inside it:** IDE DIOR/DIOW, the RTC data phase (DS), and the commands (codes 12–14).
- **No glitches:** outputs change only just after a falling edge, one CPLD clock-to-output delay later. A code changing several bits between edges can't glitch a 74HC273 latch strobe or a UART chip select.
- **(new) AS ends the cycle's outputs:** IOCTRL also takes the CPU's AS. While AS is high, an asynchronous preset holds off every output that drives read data or strobes a device: the IDE buffer enables, DIOR and DIOW, the RTC's DS and U35 enable, the UART selects and the latch strobes.
  - None of them can outlast the bus cycle, and a read that the FPGA holds to the end of the cycle ends exactly when AS negates.
  - The IDE /CS lines and the RTC's /CS are not tied to AS, because they must outlast their strobes (ATA t9; the RTC's AS must rise after DS falls).
- **The FPGA's side of the contract:** DEV_CODE and nDEV_EN must meet IOCTRL's setup and hold times at every falling edge. In practice the FPGA changes them just after rising edges, half a clock away from where IOCTRL samples.
  - To end an output on a falling edge, the FPGA removes the code before that edge.
  - To end it at AS negation, the FPGA keeps the code until after the cycle.

**(new) Commands instead of a data-bus register.** Software still sees the old register: power-off is bit 0 and software reset is bit 1, now in the FPGA's register map. The FPGA holds that register. So software resets the machine exactly as today, by writing bit 1 of that register (0x3000_0202 in today's map), and nothing changes for it.
- **On a write,** it sends code 12 or 13 for bit 0, and code 14 when bit 1 is set. Each goes out for one falling edge with nDEV_EN low.
- **What it saves:** IOCTRL needs no data lines, which frees pins 31 and 33 (one of them now takes AS).
- **What it adds:** the FPGA can make the register readable. It also knows when the CPU itself drives POWER_CONTROL_OUT high, so it can ignore `pwr_req` then (section 11).

The IDE timing choice (base or fast window, from A11) and IORDY never reach IOCTRL. The FPGA applies them through when it moves nDEV_EN and DSACK.

**Device timing (the FPGA's schedule)**

**Notation:**
- **Edges:** numbered as DEVMAP's T counter numbers them. Fn is the n-th falling CPU_CLK edge after AS asserts; F1 ends S2 and is the CPU's first DSACK sample.
- **"On Fa, off Fb":** the IOCTRL output changes just after those edges.
- **"Off at AS":** the output ends when AS negates, just after the cycle's last edge, because the FPGA keeps the code until the next edge.
- **DSACK:** the FPGA must assert DSACK before the "DSACK sampled" edge. On a read, the CPU latches the data on the next falling edge.

**Assumptions:**
- **AS timing:** the table assumes the FPGA sees AS in time to present the code for F1. If it doesn't, the whole schedule, DSACK included, moves one clock later, and nothing breaks.
- **Clock:** the times are for 16 MHz (T = 62.5 ns). The edge counts are FPGA parameters: they must grow above 16 MHz and can stay as they are at 12 MHz.

For comparison, CS1–CS2 today run IDE base cycles in 10 clocks, fast reads and writes in 5, RTC in 5, and the UART and latches at 0 wait states.

**The rules behind the numbers**
- **IDE (ATA PIO):**
  - **t1 (select to strobe):** mode 0 needs 70 ns, so the base window waits 2 clocks. Mode 2 needs 30 ns, so the fast window waits 1.
  - **Strobe widths:** mode 0 needs 290 ns: DIOW gets 5 clocks (312 ns) and DIOR 6 (375 ns). Mode 2 needs 100 ns: 2 clocks (125 ns).
  - **Reads:** the strobe ends on the edge where the CPU latches the data, so the drive still drives through the latch, and the select stays a clock longer (t9). The buffer enable ends when AS negates, so the 245s let go of the CPU bus before the next cycle (68030 #29A: within 50 ns of AS).
  - **Writes:** the strobe ends a clock before the cycle does, so the CPU's data and the select outlast it (t4, t9).
  - **Address hold after a read:** DA0–DA2 come straight from the CPU's A5–A7, so their hold is half a clock minus IOCTRL's delay, about 24 ns, as in CS2.
  - **IORDY:** in the base window the FPGA freezes the schedule while IORDY is low, as CS3 does. The fast window acknowledges before IORDY could be seen, so it ignores it (mode 2 doesn't require it).
- **RTC:**
  - **Strobe order:** nRTC_CS (the RTC's AS) leads DS by a clock and trails it by a clock, so DS always falls before AS rises. Today's logic doesn't guarantee that (TheoryOfOperations finding 15).
  - **DS width:** three clocks (187 ns), about today's length.
  - **Reads:** DS stays high through the CPU's latch edge, because the RTC only drives data while DS is high.
  - **Writes:** DS falls a clock before the cycle ends, while the CPU still drives the data.
  - **Ending a read at AS instead:** IOCTRL can also end DS at AS negation, if the FPGA keeps nDEV_EN low past F5. The data would then hold to the 68030's #29. But at 16 MHz DS would fall only about when the next cycle's R/W may change (worst case by the data sheets), so the schedule keeps F5.
  - **Check:** compare these against the DS12887 data sheet, which isn't in `datasheets/`.
- **Address and data multiplexing:** unchanged from today. U33 runs in Motorola mode (MOT high). While RTC_A_SEL is low, U34 drives CPU A0–A7 onto the RTC's AD bus, and nRTC_CS falling latches the address. While it is high, U34 is off and U35 (DIR = CPU_RW) carries the data. One code keeps nRTC_CS low through both phases, so the address can't be relatched mid-access.
- **UART** (ST16C2550 at 5V: data valid 25 ns after the access starts, no data hold (tDD min 0), address hold 5 ns, /CS and /IOW at least 40 and 25 ns):
  - **(new) Reads end at AS.** The select comes on after F1 and the FPGA keeps code 7 or 8 to F3, so the AS preset ends the read. The UART then drives until AS negates, which is the 68030's hold rule #29, and the select is off before the next cycle starts.
  - **Why not end on F3:** in the first plan the select ran to F3, into the next cycle's S0. If that cycle is a write, the UART sees /CS with its /IOW (CPU R/W) low and takes a spurious write. Whatever the next cycle is, A0–A2 change while the read is still open.
  - **Why not end on F2:** the latch edge is too early, because the UART's data could vanish with the select (no hold, against #30's 12 ns).
  - **Address hold:** the address stays valid until at least the next rising edge, 31 ns after F2 at 16 MHz. AS negates up to 20 ns after F2 and IOCTRL's preset path adds about 10 ns. So in the worst case by the data sheets the UART gets about 0 ns of its 5 ns, and typically about 15 ns. Today's board has the same race through nCPU_RD's AS preset, and works. If a scope shows trouble, the FPGA can end the select on F2 instead.
  - **Read access:** 25 ns from the select, so the 0-wait-state read has about 30 ns to spare at 16 MHz (the earlier check item is closed).
  - **Writes:** the select ends a clock before the cycle does, so the UART takes the data while the CPU still drives it (finding 13).
- **Latches:**
  - **Writes:** the 74HC273s clock on the rising edge of their strobe, which now comes a full clock before the CPU stops driving the data (finding 13).
  - **Reads:** a read of a latch address emits no code, so it no longer clocks the latch.
- **Commands (codes 12–14):** the FPGA presents the code with nDEV_EN low at F2 of the register write, chosen from D24/D25 as the CPU drives them, and the cycle ends with 1 wait state.

**Gone from the old decode**
- nINTCTRL_CS and nPTC_CS (from DEVMAP) and nINT_ACK_CYCLES (BUSCTRL → INTCTRL): inside the FPGA now.
- The five expansion selects (nEXP_8BDEV, nEXP_16BDEV, nEXP_32BDEV, nEXP_VIO_CS, nEXP_VMEM_CS): dropped. Cards decode their own windows (section 2).
- nEXP_CACHE_CTRL: dropped.
- nDEV_WAIT (DEVMAP → BUSCTRL): internal now.

**Implementation** (`fpga/ioctrl/IOCTRL_REV1.PLD`)
- **Registered selects and strobes:** each one is a flip-flop clocked on !CPU_CLK. Its next state is decoded from the DEV_CODE, nDEV_EN and R/W present at that edge, and includes FPGA_DONE:
  - nIDEx_CSA/B and nIDEx_BUFFER_EN from the code;
  - nIDE_RD for code 1–4 with nDEV_EN low and R/W high, and nIDE_WR for the same with R/W low. The IDE keeps its own DIOR because a chip-select change while DIOR is low counts as a read (it clears INTRQ or consumes data), so it can't share nCPU_RD;
  - nRTC_CS from code 5. RTC_A_SEL is code 5 with nDEV_EN low. RTC_D_SEL is now a flip-flop of its own with the opposite level, so the two change together and both end at AS;
  - nUART_CSA/B and the three latch strobes from their codes.
- **Presets:**
  - **IDE and RTC /CS:** preset off while FPGA_DONE is low.
  - **The AS-tied outputs:** preset off while nCPU_AS is high (RTC_A_SEL is cleared).
  - **No preset on both:** a macrocell's preset is a single product term, and nothing inverts after the register, so one output can't be preset on "DONE low or AS high" without an extra logic level.
  - **Why DONE still covers the AS-tied outputs:** DONE low holds the CPU in reset. The 68030 three-states AS during reset (user's manual 7.8), so AS's pull-up holds them off. That is the reason AS needs a pull-up.
- **Commands:** code 12 with nDEV_EN low sets the power-off bit and code 13 clears it. Code 14 sets a one-clock SOFT flag, which starts a system reset on the next edge.
- **Reset:**
  - **Clearing:** nSYS_RESET's register is cleared at once by FPGA_DONE low or nPWR_RESET_SINK low; the fitter builds that OR from a foldback term. SOFT clears it on the next edge.
  - **Stretch:** a 10-bit counter then runs, and one edge after it reaches all ones the register sets: 1024 clocks after the request ends. The 68030 needs 520 clocks after power-up (user's manual 7.8) and ATA drives need 25 µs; 1024 clocks cover both up to 40 MHz.
  - **(new) Release kick:** the line is driven low in reset, driven high for two clocks as it is released, then left Hi-Z with R47 holding it. The 68030's RESET input must change within 1.5 clocks (#32); R47 alone, 10K into the line's load, takes microseconds.
  - **After a RESET instruction:** IOCTRL samples the line on every falling edge, and the first edge that sees it high again drives it high for a clock. That shortens R47's slow edge.
  - **RESET instruction:** IOCTRL never treats it as a reset request, so it doesn't reset the CPU. SYS_RESET_POS, the UART reset, follows the line, so it does reset the UART.
- **Power-off request:** POWER_CONTROL_OUT is driven high while the bit is set and is Hi-Z otherwise, as INTCTRL rev 16 does. The line itself going low (any reset, including the RESET instruction) clears the bit, as INTCTRL's did.
- **(new) nCPU_RD:** NOT CPU_RW, held high while FPGA_DONE is low (section 12, power-up window).
  - **It stays immediate,** so the SRAM can answer a 2-clock synchronous read.
  - **Why rev 29 delayed it:** BUSCTRL rev 29 delayed it to S3 only because the IDE shared it: /IOR ran ahead of /CS, breaking ATA t1 (TheoryOfOperations 12.5). The IDE has its own DIOR now, so open item 6 is closed.
  - **If a device ever needs a later read strobe** (rev 29's: on at S3, off at AS negation), IOCTRL can make one on a spare pin, now that it has AS (Jeff, 21 Nov 2026). Give that device its own /OE or /IOR. The SRAM must stay on the immediate nCPU_RD for its 2-clock reads.
- **Pin-keepers:** off. DONE's 100K pull-down would fight a keeper (also about 100K) to mid-rail.
- **Spares:** pins 22 and 31 drive low; bring them to test pads.
- **Size:** 36 of 64 macrocells, 31 flip-flops and 3 foldback terms. Logic block A is full (it holds the counter); B, C and D have room.
- **Part:** the 5V ATF1504AS (the ASV is a 3.3V part). The timing here assumes the -10 grade, as the earlier CPLD analysis did (`docs/SRAMBurstProposal.md`): about 7 ns clock to output, and about 10 ns through an asynchronous preset.

**Board requirements**
- **AS and R/W:** 10K pull-ups to +5V on nCPU_AS and CPU_RW. The 68030 three-states both during reset, and the AS-tied outputs depend on AS being high then. The FPGA's U_IN4 and U_IN5 inputs don't float either.
- **DONE:** a 100K pull-down at IOCTRL pin 40, so a missing Au reads as unconfigured. The Au's 4.7K and 10K pull-ups still give about 3.2V when it is there.
- **DEV_CODE:** 10K pull-downs on DEV_CODE[3:0], as in section 9.
- **From V2:** R47 on nSYS_RESET, and J11 with R56/R57 (10K pull-ups on TDI and TMS).
- **Suggested:** two 10K bussed networks to +5V on the 16 active-low outputs, and a 10K pull-down on RTC_A_SEL. Every device then stays deselected while IOCTRL is blank or being reprogrammed in place, when its outputs float.

**Pinout** (TQFP-44, the part and package the PTC uses). The fitter placed every signal on these pins with JTAG on, with room to spare.
- **Placement is free:** IOCTRL needn't sit where U11 is today (Jeff, 21 Nov 2026). Put it where it fits the new board best, ideally close to the IDE buffers and the RTC, which take most of its lines and the tightest timing. Keep the DEV_CODE lines from the Br short, since they come straight from 3.3V FPGA pins.
- **Then fit the pins to the layout:** reshuffle them to suit the routing and rerun `check-ioctrl.ps1`, which takes seconds. Keep CPU_CLK on pin 37 (the global clock) and the JTAG pins where they are.

- JTAG, kept for in-system programming: TDI 1, TMS 7, TCK 26, TDO 32. They go to J11 pins 9, 5, 1 and 3, as on V2 (2 and 10 GND, 4 VCC, R56/R57 pull-ups).

## 11. Interrupts

- **Inputs:** nine. Eight come through U_IN6 onto the Au's LED0–7 pins: IDE1, IDE2, UART A, UART B, RTC, expansion lines 1–3. The power controller's request comes through U_IN5 on J8-9. The PTC interrupts are internal to the FPGA.
- **Not included:** the debug button (old INT7A, the level-7 NMI), dropped.
- **Power request (old INT1B):** one line, PWR_REQGR on the POWER_CONTROL_OUT net, carries both directions between the CPU and the power-controller AVR (U65; firmware in `src/Roscoe/PowerControlModule/main.c`). R9/R46 hold it low.
  - **CPU to AVR,** through IOCTRL's power-off bit: a 20–100 ms high pulse arms coordinated shutdown. Holding the line high for more than 300 ms then powers off.
  - **AVR to CPU:** once armed, a power-button press gives a 50 ms high pulse.
  - **In the FPGA:** synchronise `pwr_req` and latch its rising edge. Ignore the line while the CPU itself drives it high. The power-off bit is in the FPGA's own register, which it passes to IOCTRL as codes 12 and 13, so the FPGA always knows when that is.
  - **INTCTRL rev 17 never enabled it:** mask bit INT_MSK0 is fixed at 0. Its flop also clocks on the line's falling edge, although the comments say rising.
- **Expansion wiring (Jeff, 22 Nov 2026):** three of today's seven expansion interrupt lines stay, still bussed to all three slots as all seven are today, so a card that does two jobs can use two lines.
  - Line 1 is connector B21 (old nEXP_INT3A), line 2 is A23 (old nEXP_INT3B) and line 3 is A24 (old nEXP_INT2A, which the slot symbol calls EXP_nVIDRFSH_INT). Each keeps its 10K pull-up to +5V (R58, R20, R22) and reaches the FPGA through U_IN6.
  - Each line keeps its old mask bit.
  - Cards that share a line must drive it open-drain, and the handler then asks each card.
  - The other four lines (A22, A29, B22, B23) are unused (section 15).
- **Vectoring:** all interrupts are vectored, as today. AVEC is only a pull-up (R49) on the current board. The FPGA puts the vector on D24–D31 during interrupt acknowledge; the interrupt level comes from A1–A3.
- **Level and vector assignment (agreed 22 Nov 2026):** every source that remains keeps its INTCTRL rev 17 level, vector and mask bit, so the existing software works unchanged (table below). The vector is built as today: 1, then the level (3 bits), then a one-hot source (4 bits). Every other vector of the form 1xxxxxxx should go to a spurious-interrupt handler, as rev 16's comments say.
  - **The expansion lines** are new: all three at level 3 (table below).
  - **Later:** the UARTs could move above the IDE. If the IDE handler moves a whole sector inside the interrupt, a UART FIFO can overrun meanwhile, and the disk doesn't mind waiting. They stay where they are for now so the existing software works; in the FPGA it is a small change later (Jeff, 22 Nov 2026).
- **Bug in INTCTRL rev 16, still in rev 17 (line 430 in both):** `INT2A_REQUEST_PE = INT3A_REQUEST_LATCHED;` should be `INT2A_REQUEST_LATCHED`.
  - **Effect:** when level 2 wins, the INT2A source bit follows INT3A, which is normally clear at that point. So an INT2A request alone gets vector 0xA0, the spurious vector, instead of 0xA1; with INT2B also pending, INT2B's 0xA2 wins.
  - Don't carry it over when porting to HDL.
- **Pin placement and the LEDs:** LED0 IDE1, LED1 IDE2, LED2 UART A, LED3 UART B, LED4 RTC, LED5–7 expansion lines 1–3. Each LED shows its line's level: an active-high request (IDE, UART) lights it, and an active-low one (RTC, expansion) puts it out. With nothing pending, LED4–7 are lit and LED0–3 are dark.
- **Qwiic:** of the Qwiic-shared pins (B33/B35, holes J11-7/J12-7), J12-7 carries A15 since 22 Nov 2026, so the Au's Qwiic connector must stay unplugged.

**Assignment (agreed 22 Nov 2026).** The masks stay where they are today: bits 15–8 at 0x3000_0200 and bits 7–0 at 0x3000_0201, all cleared by reset.

- **RTC:** a level, not an edge. Rev 16's and rev 17's comments say falling edge, but both decode `!vINT1A` as a level. That suits the DS12887: its /IRQ stays low until software reads register C.
- **Power controller:** rev 17 forces its mask bit to 0; here it is writable. It still resets to 0, so the interrupt stays off until software sets the bit, and the AVR only sends a request once the CPU has armed it.
- **Gone:** INT7A, the debug button (level 7, 0xF0, mask bit 15), and four of the seven bussed expansion lines: INT5A, 5D, 4C and 2B (slot pins A29, A22, B22 and B23; mask bits 12, 9, 6 and 2). Rev 17 forced all seven expansion masks to 0. INT3A, INT3B and INT2A carry on as expansion lines 1–3, on their old pins and mask bits 5, 4 and 3, so bits 15, 12, 9, 6 and 2 are unused. The VGA card's schematic (`schematic/VGA/VideoAdapterRoscoe.net`) connects none of the seven pins, so no card relies on them.
- **Expansion lines:** all three at level 3 (Jeff, 22 Nov 2026), below the UARTs and above the RTC; nothing else uses levels 2 or 3. Line 1 wins a tie, then line 2. A per-line level register would be an easy later addition.

The power-off and software-reset register stays at 0x3000_0202, bits 0 and 1. The FPGA keeps it and passes the bits to IOCTRL as codes 12–14 (section 10).

**HDL: `hdl/intctrl.v` (written 22 Nov 2026; simulated, not yet run on hardware).** INTCTRL rev 17 ported to Verilog, with the table above as its default.
- **Clock:** everything runs on one clock, `clk`. The FPGA's clock plan is still open (section 15, item 2), and only the power filter depends on the frequency (`CLK_HZ`).
- **Its side of the bus controller:**
  - **Register port:** a one-clock write strobe, `reg_addr` = A2–A0, 8-bit data on D31–D24, and combinational read data.
  - **Acknowledge port:** `iack_stb` for one clock as an acknowledge cycle starts, with `iack_level` = A3–A1. The vector is valid from the next clock until the next strobe.
  - **Left to the bus controller:** decoding the acknowledge cycle, driving the vector on D24–D31, and ending the cycle with DSACK0 (an 8-bit port).
- **Registers,** as offsets from 0x3000_0200:
  - 0 and 1 are the masks, now readable.
  - 4 and 5 are a read-only request register in the same bit layout, at 0x3000_0204/0205 (register map agreed 22 Nov 2026, section 16). A bit is set while a level source's line is asserted, whatever its mask bit, or while an edge source's latch is set. What the controller presents is the request register ANDed with the mask.
  - 2 and 3 are not in the module. The power-off/software-reset register belongs with the logic that sends the IOCTRL commands, and its power-off bit comes back in as `pwr_drive`.
- **Edge sources:** an edge is latched only while its mask bit is set, as on rev 17.
  - Clearing the mask bit now drops a latched edge too. On rev 17 such an edge still interrupted after its mask bit was cleared: for example after the boot loader clears both masks before starting a program.
  - An edge in the same clock as its own acknowledge is kept.
- **Vector:** captured as the acknowledge starts and held for the rest of the cycle. Rev 17 decoded it live through the cycle.
- **Power request:** it counts once the line has been low for 1 ms and then high for 1 ms (`PWR_FILTER_US`), with `pwr_drive` clear throughout. That ignores the CPU's own pulses, the slow fall through R9/R46 after IOCTRL lets go, and glitches. The AVR's pulse is 50 ms.
- **IPL timing:** with `clk` = CPU_CLK, IPL changes just after a rising edge, half a clock from the CPU's sample, as on rev 17. If `clk` becomes a multiple of CPU_CLK, give the IPL register an enable at that phase.
- **Checks:** `hdl/check-hdl.ps1` runs `hdl/tb_intctrl.v` in xsim (the first numbers below are from synthesising the module on its own for the Au's part; section 16 has the whole design).
  - **Simulation:** the testbench makes 4,249 checks. Its source table is copied from this section, not from the module, and ten bugs planted in copies of the module were each caught. It covers:
    - each source alone, and the priorities across and within levels;
    - spurious acknowledges;
    - the edge rules and the vector hold;
    - the power filter and reset;
    - 4,000 random steps against a model.
  - **Synthesis:** 47 LUTs and 60 flip-flops, with 6.5 ns of slack at 100 MHz and no warnings.

## 12. Electrical and signal-integrity rules

- **Not 5V tolerant:** every FPGA pin goes through a translator, except outputs that drive TTL-threshold inputs directly: DEV_CODE[3:0], nDEV_EN and DONE into IOCTRL.
- **Ground on the 0.1" headers is thin:** 8 grounds for 91 bank-header signals. The control headers have no ground at all, so only the slow interrupt inputs use them.
  - Keep the XDC defaults of LVCMOS33, `SLEW SLOW`, `DRIVE 8` on all outputs.
  - Put 33Ω series resistors on translator outputs into the FPGA.
  - Place the translators right at the headers, over solid ground.
  - **Fallback** if crosstalk or ground bounce shows up: mount the Hirose DF40 mates directly on Roscoe instead of using the Br. That gives a ground pair every third row, but means 0.4 mm-pitch soldering.
- **Clock:** CPU_CLK goes through the 74LVC1G34, then 33Ω, to J8-8. J7-8 (its partner) stays empty. J8-9 carries only the power request, a static line, through 1K. J7-7/J7-9 (diagonal to it) are spare, for slow or static signals only.
- **Configuration float:** PUDC_B is tied high on the Au, so all pins float during configuration. Every active-low output needs its 10K pull-up to 3V3_FPGA, so strobes stay negated while it loads.
- **Power-up window:** 3V3_FPGA comes from the Au, so until the Au's regulators come up after +5V, or whenever the Au is unplugged, those pull-ups sit at 0V.
  - The 5V AHCT244 buffers then see low inputs and assert every strobe behind them: RAS, CAS, DRAM_WE, the DRAM buffer enable, the SRAM and flash selects, IPL and BERR. (The 68882's select comes from U_FPUCS, which doesn't depend on 3V3_FPGA.)
  - IOCTRL holds reset through this (DONE is low). It also holds nCPU_RD high, so the SRAM and flash can't drive the data bus even though their selects are asserted.
  - The DRAM buffers' DIR follows R/W. The CPU floats R/W during reset, but its new pull-up makes it read, so the buffers drive the CPU bus from the DRAM side, where nothing else is driving.
  - The flash needs a command sequence before it will program. So the window should be harmless.
  - If it isn't, IOCTRL's spare pin 22 can still drive the four AHCT244 /OE pins from DONE, with pull-ups to +5V on their outputs. U_DATA and U_CPU are unpowered then, so their outputs are already off.
- **Selectable bank (34):** 18 of our signals use it. Leave VBSEL_A/B (Br J19-4, J20-4) unconnected so it stays at 3.3V.
- **Bank 15 pins (B46/B48):** run at 1.35V; leave them unconnected.
- **Reset sequencing:** IOCTRL holds SYS_RESET until DONE rises, plus a 1024-clock stretch, and drives the line high for two clocks as it releases it (68030 #32). A JTAG reload while the machine is running drops DONE and resets the machine cleanly.
- **Code-bus timing:** IOCTRL samples DEV_CODE and nDEV_EN on falling CPU_CLK edges, so the FPGA launches them from rising edges. Its path is the Au's output buffer, the header and IOCTRL's input, against half a clock (31 ns at 16 MHz). Checked 22 Nov 2026 with the registers in the I/O blocks: 7.3 ns to spare against IOCTRL's 7 ns setup, worst case (section 16).
- **Missing FPU:** the FPGA must not drive DSACK/STERM during FPU cycles. It must assert BERR on an unanswered coprocessor cycle so the 68030 takes the F-line exception.
- **Flash writes are CE#-controlled:** the flash's /WE is CPU R/W and its /OE is nCPU_RD, so nFLASH_CE is the write strobe. From the SST39SF040 data sheet (table 12):
  - CE# low for at least 40 ns (TCP), and still low 40 ns after the CPU's write data is valid (TDS).
  - CE# high again before R/W rises (TCH = 0), and at least 10 ns before /OE falls (TOEH).
  - At least 30 ns between CE# pulses (TCPH); back-to-back bus cycles give more.

  Ending CE# a clock before the cycle ends on writes, as the other write strobes in section 10 do, covers the second point with a clock to spare.
- **SRAM synchronous cycles and bursts:** `docs/SRAMBurstProposal.md` has the same budget for the CPLD board.
  - **68030 limits (20 MHz grade):** STERM and CBACK need 4 ns setup and 12 ns hold at the rising edge (#60/#61). Read data needs 4 ns setup and 12 ns hold at the following falling edge (#27/#30). The fitted grade isn't recorded, and faster grades need less.
  - **The FPGA's paths:** the CPU's address is valid by 25 ns (#6). Then come U_IN (about 5 ns), the FPGA pin to pin (about 8 ns), and U_CPU (about 5 ns) or an AHCT244 (about 8 ns), plus the headers. That's about 9 ns more than the proposal's -10 CPLD. Vivado now gives the FPGA's share (section 16, "I/O timing"): 6.9 ns from A3:A2 to SRAM_BA and 8.7 to CIIN, worst case.
  - **First long word:** STERM is seen at the rising edge that starts S2, and the data is latched at the falling edge that ends it.
    - STERM is out at about 45 ns, against a deadline of T − 4: about 13 ns of margin at 16 MHz, but about 1 ns at 20 MHz.
    - SRAM data is valid at about 60 ns (CE and BA2/BA3 out at about 48, plus 12), against 1.5T − 4: about 30 ns of margin at 16 MHz, 11 ns at 20 MHz.
    - So the first word runs at 2 clocks at 16 MHz. At 20 MHz it needs fast slew on STERM, or one wait state (3-1-1-1).
    - IOCTRL's nCPU_RD (NOT R/W, immediate) already gives the early /OE this needs.
  - **Later long words:** the counter must step after the previous word's 12 ns hold, and early enough to leave tAA (12 ns) before the next latch edge.
    - Clocked from an MMCM output a quarter clock behind the latch edge, the new data is valid about 27 ns after the step. That leaves about 16 ns of margin at 16 MHz and 6 ns at 20 MHz, with about 10 ns of hold margin.
    - The CPLD version could only step on the latch edge itself, so its hold margin couldn't be shown on paper.
  - **Rules:** never assert STERM together with DSACK. Decode SRAM reads from the address alone, as the proposal does; the CPU ignores STERM until AS is asserted.

- **DRAM:** U26–U28 are SN74AHC157 address muxes (CMOS thresholds). U29–U32 are 74HCT245 data buffers with /OE on GND and DIR = nDRAM_DATA_EN, the CPU on their B side; BUSCTRL drives DIR high only for DRAM reads. SIMMs are U18–U21. Y1 is a 20 MHz oscillator.
- **Pull-ups on the CPU control lines:** DSACK0/1 have R51/R52, HALT has R2, AVEC has R49 and SYS_RESET has R47. BERR, STERM, CIIN and IPL0–2 have none; BUSCTRL and INTCTRL drove them push-pull.

- **Flash:** U3 is an SST39SF040. Its /WE is nBFLASH_WE from BUSCTRL pin 40, the flash decode with R/W low, so it falls and rises with /CE. The SRAM connector's nWR (U6 B32) is already plain CPU R/W.

- **Slow-device logic families:**
  - IDE: 74AHC244 (U39, U42) and SN74AHC245 (U37, U38, U40, U41).
  - RTC: 74AHC244 (U34) and SN74AHC245 (U35).
  - Latches: 74HC273 (U7, U9, U10), all three on D24–D31 and cleared by nSYS_RESET.
  - Their inputs have CMOS thresholds, so they need 5V drive.
- **PTC (U11):** a 16-bit device on D16–D31 with A1 as register select. Timer A was clocked by oscillator U12; timer B by CPU_CLK_DIV16 from BUSCTRL. J11 is its JTAG header.
- **Power controller:** an AVR (U65), with these nets:
  - PWR_REQGR, on the POWER_CONTROL_OUT net (INTCTRL pin 45 out, pin 30 in). R9/R46 (10K each) pull it low, so nothing reaches the CPLD while the main power is off and the AVR runs on standby.
  - RESET_SINK (to BUSCTRL nSYS_RESET_IN).
  - The reset and power buttons.
  - PS_ON and PWR_OK to the ATX supply.
- **Software reset:** RESET_CONTROL ran INTCTRL → BUSCTRL.
- **DEVMAP outputs to devices:**
  - IDE: CSA/CSB and buffer enable for each channel, plus a shared nIDE_WR.
  - RTC: nRTC_CS (to /CS and AS), RTC_A_SEL (to DS and U34), RTC_D_SEL (to U35).
  - UART: CSA/CSB, plus SYS_RESET_POS.
  - The 7-segment A/B and status-LED latch strobes.

## 16. FPGA HDL: the bus controller

**Status (22 Nov 2026):** `hdl/roscoe_top.v`, `hdl/busctrl.v` and `hdl/dramctrl.v` are written, and wired to `hdl/intctrl.v` (section 11). They pass `hdl/check-hdl.ps1` (below), including every I/O path against the 68030's and the translators' data-sheet timing (`roscoe_timing.xdc`, "I/O timing" below). They haven't run on hardware. Still missing: the SRAM's synchronous cycles and bursts, and the redesigned PTC. The 68882's select left the FPGA for a '138 on the 5V side (below).

**Clocking (agreed 22 Nov 2026).** Everything runs on cpu_clk, on both edges, as the CPLDs did.
- **Edge numbering, as in section 10:**
  - Rk is the k-th rising edge of a cycle, and R1 the first one that sees AS asserted.
  - Fk is the falling edge after Rk, so F1 ends S2 and is the CPU's first DSACK sample.
  - A registered output that must be seen at Fk changes at Rk.
- **Decisions at R1:** they come straight from the AS, address, FC, SIZ and R/W pins, which the 68030 holds stable at that edge. If AS misses R1, the whole cycle runs a clock later. Vivado checks that it doesn't (below).
- **AS last:** every register's next value is first worked out as if AS were asserted, from the address and the registered state, and AS (with reset) gates it at the very end (`go` in `busctrl.v` and `dramctrl.v`). AS arrives up to 26 ns into the half clock before R1, so it passes through one or two LUTs at most.

**Memory map:** as BUSCTRL rev 31 and DEVMAP rev 24 (the table at the top of `busctrl.v`), with these differences (card windows agreed 22 Nov 2026, section 2):
- **0x3 region:** the on-board devices keep 0x3000_0000–0x3000_08FF. 0x3000_0400 is the new PTC's window, acknowledged at once until it exists. The rest of 0x3 is 8-bit card space ended by nEXP_ACK; before, the empty on-board windows were acknowledged at once.
- **VIO window (0x3000_05xx):** 8-bit card space that also drives nEXP_VIO_CS.
- **Old PTC window (0x4000_0400):** acknowledged at once, like the other empty 16-bit on-board windows.
- **VMEM window:** the first 16 MB of 0x5 (0x5000_0000–0x50FF_FFFF; 2 MB before). It is ordinary 32-bit card space ended by nEXP_ACK, and it also drives nEXP_VMEM_CS.
- **The two card selects:** for cards that don't decode the address themselves (the VGA card).
  - They are registered at R1 from the decode, like the memory selects, and cut by the AS pin, like U_DATA's enable. So they are on from about R1 + 15 ns to about AS + 25 ns at the card.
  - A card that answers from its select takes 2 wait states at best. Its nEXP_ACK can't reach the FPGA before R2.
- **Boot-flash reads:** 1 wait state (`FLASH_RD_WS`; rev 31 used 0; agreed 22 Nov 2026). The FPGA's registered CE comes about 10 ns after BUSCTRL's, which would leave a 70 ns flash only a few nanoseconds at 0 wait states.
- **Flash writes:** 1 wait state, with CE ending at R2 (section 12).
- **SRAM:** 0 wait states as today, but a write now ends CE at R2, a clock before the cycle ends, instead of at AS negation.

**Ending a cycle**
- **DSACK:** asserted from its edge until the rising edge that sees AS negated. It is then driven negated for half a clock, and three-stated at the next falling edge. It is never driven in CPU-space cycles other than interrupt acknowledge, so the 68882 has it to itself. U_CPU's enable, nDSACK_OE, comes from an ODDR in the pin's I/O block, so both its edges leave from the pin itself.
- **BERR:** asserted at R1 for 0x2. Otherwise the bus timeout asserts it 240 rising edges after R1 (`TIMEOUT_CLKS`), counting even while IORDY holds the IDE schedule. It catches 0x6–0x7, empty card space, breakpoint cycles and a missing FPU. It is held until AS negates.
- **CIIN:** asserted for 0x3–0x5, decoded from A31–A28 alone without a register. CIIN is a synchronous input, so the CPU wants it stable at every rising edge while AS is asserted, R1 included (data sheet note 13). A register at R1 would be too late for R1. In CPU space A31–A28 are never 3–5, so FC isn't needed.
- **Card cycles:** nEXP_ACK passes through one flip-flop and then DSACK, so the cycle takes 1 wait state at best.

**Device codes:** section 10's schedule, edge for edge.
- **IORDY hold:** in the base IDE window, the schedule stops at positions 6 and 7 (the steps to F7 and F8) while IORDY, through a two-flop synchroniser, is low.
- **When IORDY first counts:** the first sample used is taken at R5, a clock and a half after IOCTRL asserts the strobe, so it is well past ATA's tA.

**FPGA registers, 0x3000_0200–020F (agreed 22 Nov 2026)**

All are 8-bit and take 0 wait states, except writes to 0x202. Reset clears every writable bit.

| Address | Register | Access |
|---|---|---|
| 0x200, 0x201 | Interrupt masks, sources 15–8 and 7–0 (section 11) | read/write |
| 0x202 | Bit 0 power-off request, bit 1 software reset | write: IOCTRL command; read: bit 0 |
| 0x203 | FPGA design version (`FPGA_VERSION`, 0x01 for this revision) | read only |
| 0x204, 0x205 | Interrupt requests, sources 15–8 and 7–0 | read only |
| 0x206 | Control: bit 0 SRAM synchronous cycles (STERM), bit 1 SRAM burst fills (CBACK) | read/write |
| 0x207 | Scratch, no effect | read/write |
| 0x208–0x20F | free | read 0, writes ignored |

- **0x202:**
  - A write sends code 12 or 13 for bit 0, or code 14 when bit 1 is set, at F2, with 1 wait state. If both bits are set, bit 1 wins: the reset clears the power-off bit anyway.
  - A read returns the power-off bit, which also feeds `pwr_drive`.
- **0x203:** bump `FPGA_VERSION` (a `busctrl.v` parameter) with each released bitstream, so software can report which one is loaded.
- **0x206:** both bits start at 0, so the SRAM runs today's DSACK cycles until software turns the faster ones on. They do nothing until the SRAM step is written.
- **0x207:** for testing D24–D31 and U_DATA; the testbench walks a one and a zero through it.
- **A4–A7:** these don't reach the FPGA, so the 16 bytes repeat through the 256-byte window.

**D24–D31 (U_DATA)**
- **Reads:** for reads of its registers and for interrupt acknowledge, the FPGA's drivers and U_DATA open at R1. The read data is registered in the I/O blocks at every rising edge, so the value the CPU latches at F2 left the FPGA at R2 and only U_DATA's delay stands between them. For an acknowledge, that is the vector `intctrl.v` latches at R2.
- **Writes:** U_DATA opens from R1 for writes to the FPGA's registers, and the FPGA takes the data at R2.
- **Release:** the AS pin itself cuts both drivers, so they let go as AS negates (#29).
- **Scope check:** U_DATA's DIR is R/W. If the next cycle's R/W changed before U_DATA's /OE rose, U_DATA would briefly drive the FPGA's side. Cutting at AS keeps that window to a few nanoseconds even in the worst case by the data sheets. Look at it on the first board.

**DRAM (`dramctrl.v`)**
- **Access:** 4 clocks with 1 wait state, as today.
  - RAS falls on the addressed rank at R1, and DRAM_MUX switches to the column address at F1.
  - CAS falls at R2, on all lanes for a read and the written lanes for a write, and DSACK is asserted then too.
  - RAS rises at R3. CAS stays low until AS negates.
  - WE is low from R1 on a write.
  - The 245s' new /OE is on from R1, and AS cuts it, so the transceivers stop driving at once. That removes the end-of-read overlap described in `docs/DRAM.md` 3.3.
  - At 16 MHz that gives tRAS 125 ns, tRCD 62.5, tRAH over 31 and tRSH 62.5, and tRP 125 between back-to-back accesses.
- **Ranks:** 64 MB windows (`RANK_64MB` = 1, J3 = 1-2) or 16 MB. `ras_n[0..7]` are ranks 1A, 2A, 3A, 4A, 1B, 2B, 3B, 4B in address order, the same as BUSCTRL rev 31's nDRAM_S1A … S4B. So the new board's RAS0–7 wire up as today: rank A to a SIMM's RAS0 and RAS2, rank B to RAS1 and RAS3.
- **Refresh:** CAS before RAS, requested every `REFRESH_NS` = 15.6 µs (249 clocks at 16 MHz), against rev 31's 128 clocks.
  - **When it starts:** between bus cycles, or during any cycle that can't be a DRAM cycle. It never cuts into a DRAM cycle.
  - **Sequence:** CAS is low from the first rising edge after the start to the third, and RAS from the second to the fourth. That gives tCSR 62.5 ns, tCHR 62.5 and tRAS 125. A waiting access starts at least a clock (tRP) after RAS rises.
  - **During refresh:** WE stays high and the 245s stay off.
  - **Through resets:** the timer keeps running through system resets and the 68030's RESET instruction, which rev 31's didn't.
  - **8K-refresh parts:** 15.6 µs suits 2K/32 ms and 4K/64 ms parts. Some 64 Mbit chips on 128 MB SIMMs are 8K/64 ms and need 7.8 µs, so check the chip markings and set `REFRESH_NS` to match.

**I/O timing (`roscoe_timing.xdc`, 22 Nov 2026)**
- **What the file holds:** hand-written. `gen-xdc.ps1` never touches it, and the header says how each number is worked out.
  - Input and output delays come from the 68030's AC table (1991 data sheet, 20 MHz column, the slowest grade it lists) and the translators' data-sheet maxima at 50 pF and full temperature: 74LVC244A/245A 5.9 ns (8.0 to enable), 74AHCT244 8.5, AHC157 11.0. Recheck those against the parts' own sheets, which aren't in `datasheets/` yet.
  - They also include 1 ns of wiring each way and U_CLK's skew: the FPGA's clock edges come 1–4 ns after the CPU's.
  - Y1's 45–55 % duty cycle takes 3.1 ns off every half-clock path.
  - Pin-to-pin limits cover what is decoded straight from the pins: CIIN (at the CPU by R1) and SRAM_BA. They also cover AS turning off U_DATA, the FPGA's data drivers, the 245s (#29A) and the card selects.
- **Results at 16 MHz** (worst case for every part, Vivado's slow corner):

| Path | Must arrive by | Worst slack, ns |
|---|---|---|
| AS into the R1 registers | R1 | +0.90 |
| Address, FC, SIZ, R/W into the R1 registers | R1 | +7.94 |
| Write data D31–D24 | R2 | +30.1 |
| DSACK0/1 and nDSACK_OE, at the CPU | F1 − 4 ns (#47A) | +2.31 |
| BERR, at the CPU | F1 − 4 ns | +0.56 |
| Read data, at the CPU | F2 − 4 ns (#27) | +3.37 |
| DEV_CODE, nDEV_EN, at IOCTRL | the falling edge − 7 ns | +7.28 |
| RAS, CAS, DRAM_WE, nDRAM_BUF_OE, past U_DRAM1/2 | the next falling edge | +1.47 |
| DRAM_MUX, through the AHC157 | CAS at the next rising edge | +6.44 |
| SRAM and flash selects, past U_MEM1/2 | the next falling edge | +2.19 |
| nEXP_VMEM_CS, nEXP_VIO_CS, past U_MEM2 (and their AS cut) | the next falling edge | +2.56 |
| CIIN, SRAM_BA | R1 | +7.94 |

  - **Hold:** at least 25 ns on every I/O path that keeps a hold check, and 0.13 ns on the tightest internal one.
  - **The FPGA, pin to pin:** 6.9 ns for SRAM_BA (in and out in one bank), 8.8 for CIIN, 8.6 for AS to nDATA_OE, 8.7 and 9.4 for AS to the card selects, and 10.5 for AS to nDRAM_BUF_OE (across the die). nFPU_CS took 10.0 before it left the FPGA. Of each, 1.4 ns is the input buffer and 3.7 the output buffer (SLOW, 8 mA). The 8 ns section 12 assumed was about right.
  - **At 20 MHz** the half clock is 6.25 ns shorter, so the AS, DSACK, BERR and select paths would all fail by 3–6 ns. A faster clock needs the MMCM or wait states.
- **What it took** (all simulated again afterwards):
  - "AS last" (above): AS → R1 had six LUT levels and failed by 2 ns.
  - nDSACK_OE through an ODDR: it had been the OR of a rising-edge and a falling-edge flop, far from its pin (−6 ns).
  - Registers in the I/O blocks for DSACK, DEV_CODE, nDEV_EN, DRAM_MUX, IPL and the read data. BERR and the memory selects stay in the fabric, between AS and their pins on the far side of the die.
  - Read data registered, with the drivers and U_DATA opening at R1 instead of R2 (see "D24–D31").
  - CIIN from A31–A28 alone (see "Ending a cycle").
  - **Pin moves (Jeff's go-ahead):**
    - the half-clock outputs into bank 35 next to AS, and A8–A15 and A24 out of their way;
    - then nFPU_CS with FC1, and SRAM_BA2/3, beside their inputs (sections 6 and 7). nFPU_CS later left the FPGA altogether (below). FC1 stayed with the other CPU-space inputs.
    - Before the second set, nFPU_CS took 20.7 ns and SRAM_BA 18.0 ns. Crossing the die cost a few ns of that. Most was router padding (next point).
  - **No same-edge hold on the address, FC, SIZ, R/W and write-data inputs:**
    - With no MMCM, the clock reaches the flip-flops about 4.5 ns after the FPGA's clock pin. The inputs' minimum delay is below zero, so Vivado wants every address net several ns longer to meet hold at the edge where the address changes.
    - Nothing depends on that edge. The CPU changes these lines only after the rising edge that starts S0, with AS negated. There every register that takes them is either gated off by AS or loaded again at R1, and write data is only taken at R2.
    - So `roscoe_timing.xdc` drops those hold checks, and says why. The padding had also been slowing nFPU_CS and SRAM_BA, which share the nets.
  - **No hold on nDSACK_OE's falling edge:** that edge only releases a DSACK already driven high, after AS has negated (#28).
- **The tightest margins:** BERR (+0.56) and AS → R1 (+0.90) are the paths to watch when the logic changes. If BERR goes negative, move it into bank 35, where its flip-flop can go in the I/O block (the spare J11-7 hole).

**The 68882's select (decided 22 Nov 2026: U_FPUCS, on the 5V side)**
- **Why it left the FPGA:** the FPGA decoded nFPU_CS from FC0–2 and A19–A16 alone, as BUSCTRL rev 31 does. That met the 68882's CS-to-DS setup on writes (#8B, 35 ns) with about 7 ns to spare. But CS changes only when the address does.
  - After an FPU cycle, the next cycle's address can come 25 ns into S0 (#6). It then took about 26 ns more to reach the 68882: U_IN, 10 ns in the FPGA, U_MEM2 and wiring.
  - The next AS can fall as early as 31 ns after S0 (3 ns after the falling edge, at 45 % duty).
  - So CS could stay low for up to about 20 ns of a following non-FPU cycle's AS. The 68882 manual (figure 10-5) calls that incorrect.
  - Qualifying CS with AS, or registering it, would keep it clear of the next cycle, but would assert it too late for #8B: only 12–14 ns before DS.
- **Now:** a '138 on the CPU's own lines makes it (section 9). With the decoder's delay tPD, counted from the edge that starts S0 (the AC table in `datasheets/MC68881_MC68882_Floating-Point_Coprocessor_Users_Manual_1ed_1987.pdf`, 16.67 MHz column; the 68030's 20 MHz column):
  - **Overlap into a following AS:** up to tPD − 6 ns. That's about 4 ns for a 10 ns part, as rev 31's ATF1508 (-10) has, and 9 ns for a 15 ns part. Only a decoder under about 6 ns could rule it out, since the 68030 itself leaves just 6 ns between its latest address change and its earliest AS. Typical delays give none.
  - **CS 35 ns before DS on writes (#8B):** 33.6 − tPD to spare, about 24 ns for a 10 ns part.
  - **CS held 10 ns after AS and DS negate (#9, #9A):** AS can negate 20 ns after the falling edge, and the next address can change right at the rising edge 28 ns after it. That leaves 8 ns, so the decoder's minimum delay must supply the last 2. The ATF22V10C guarantees 3.0 ns. An AHCT '138 is only specified down to about 1 ns, which is about 1 ns short on paper in that extreme corner.
  - **Which part (Jeff, 22 Nov 2026):** a '138, AHCT, ACT or F. An ATF22V10C-10 would meet every number on paper, but draws 85–130 mA; a -15 would double the overlap. With an AHCT part, look at CS against AS negation on the first board, since its 1 ns minimum is the one figure short on paper.
